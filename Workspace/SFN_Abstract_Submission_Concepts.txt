2026 Abstract Concept 2: Spasticity is a debilitating sensorimotor disorder and is driven in part by maladaptive plasticity that produces hyperreflexia, hyperreflexia is a major contributor to spasticity and represents a tractable neurophysiological target for spasticity reduction.  Rehabilitative protocols such as H-reflex operant conditioning (HROC) have been shown to induce targeted reductions in spinal reflex excitability (Hyperreflexia), however, these protocols have lengthy therapeutic timelines (50 days pre-clinically, 30 sessions over 3 months clinically) and have variable outcomes. Closed-loop vagus nerve stimulation (CLV) has emerged as an adjuvant to rehabilitative therapy to enhance experience-dependent plasticity via triggering the global-phasic release of plasticity-inducing neuromodulators, including acetylcholine and norepinephrine, which provide a reinforcing signal. Herein we present an opportunity to pair CLV with H-reflex down-conditioning trials to reduce reflex excitability. We hypothesize that VNS-contingent reinforcement will accelerate and amplify reflex down-conditioning in an in-vivo pre-clinical model of HROC in awake-behaving rats. HROC is uniquely suited for pairing with CLV because it directly targets the spinal reflex circuitry believed to generate hyperreflexia and may result in a targeted therapy for hyperreflexia and spasticity.
To address this, we constructed a pre-clinical system capable of simultaneous multi-channel recording and stimulation alongside a custom python software suite and instrumented hardware, capable performing reflex conditioning experiments combined with CLV delivery contingent upon successful H-reflex down-conditioning trials based on a defined reward criterion (H-reflex size reduction). In the experimental set-up, we stimulate the tibial nerve to elicit a stretch reflex (H-reflex) and record the soleus emg response with implanted stainless steel electrodes. The experiment is probing the response, the H-reflex emg response provides a widely used measure of spinal reflex pathway excitability. under different controlled conditions and modifying the H-reflex with conditioning principles. In addition to this, this system is also equipped to precisely deliver food pellet (standard) or vagal nerve stimulation (active) upon successful trials. This is a synergistic approach that combines directed plasticity effects of H-reflex Operant Conditioning (HROC) with the enhancing plasticity effects of Closed-loop Vagus nerve stimulation (CLV) therapies.  
The expected outcome is a reduction in the H-reflex size, and have that reduction augmented when the food pellet reward is replaced with VNS delivery.  We expect this combinatorial strategy to yield superior outcomes compared to rehabilitation or CLV alone, offering a more effective, targeted intervention to selectively treat spasticity.


2026 Abstract Concept1: Spasticity is a debilitating sensorimotor disorder characterized by hyperactive stretch reflexes (hyperreflexia) and velocity-dependent hypertonia, resulting from upper motor neuron (UMN) injury. New insights that spasticity arises from abnormal plastic rearrangement, specifically an imbalance between descending inhibitory and facilitatory regulation of spinal stretch reflexes. Recovery from such neurological injury requires leveraging the inherent plasticity of the central nervous system to precisely rewire and reshape these damaged neural circuits.[YS1.1] To address this, we propose Vagus Nerve Stimulation-enhanced H-reflex Operant Conditioning (VeHROC), a synergistic approach that combines directed plasticity with enhancer plasticity therapies. 
In this paradigm, H-reflex operant conditioning (HROC) serves as a targeted training protocol to down-regulate the hyperactive reflex pathways that are the primary contributors to spasticity. HROC, however, is a gradual process typically conducted over approximately three months clinically (30 sessions) or 50 days in animal models and requires active subject participation and motivation. Because HROC relies on operant principles, it traditionally uses external rewards such as food pellets for animals or visual cues for humans, to drive neural change.
We hypothesize this directed change from reflex conditioning will be augmented by closed-loop vagus nerve stimulation (CLV) which delivers brief bursts of stimulation timed to coincide with successful conditioning trials. Crucially, VNS can engage neuromodulatory systems associated with reinforcement and plasticity by triggering the phasic release of plasticity-inducing neuromodulators globally, including acetylcholine and norepinephrine. This engagement of neuromodulatory centers (the locus coeruleus and nucleus basalis) provides a reinforcing signal to the central nervous system by synchronizing this neuromodulator release with specific neural activity. Clinically, CLV has shown efficacious results to reinforce only the specific neural networks actively engaged during the task. 
We expect this combinatorial strategy to yield superior outcomes compared to rehabilitation or CLV alone, offering a more effective, targeted intervention to selectively treat spasticity.

2025 Previous Abstract: The CNS is capable of change (plasticity) throughout life. This knowledge enables development of novel therapies for addressing neurological impairments. These protocols target beneficial change to a CNS site important in a behavior like walking or reach-and-grasp. The targeted change enables better practice, which in turn leads to widespread beneficial change that improves the entire behavior. Our pre-clinical development of these therapies typically entails studies with freely-moving chronically-implanted rats. These studies need specialized software to collect, evaluate, and store data continuously (24/7) and give immediate feedback (e.g., food pellet, electrical stimulation) to multiple animals concurrently. We address this need with our custom software Elizan. The first version of Elizan was written in 1984 to run on a DEC 11/34 computer; it supported concurrent interaction with and recording from up to 8 animals. It was modified in 1995 and again in 2010 to support 20 animals using Windows-based PCs with a SQL database for 24/7 data storage. In 2024, it was made hardware-independent. Now, the University of Texas at Dallas and the National Center for Adaptive Neurotechnologies at the Stratton VA in Albany, NY are updating Elizan to use a modern programming environment (Python), and open-source data acquisition hardware (Open Ephys) that digitizes data at a skull-mounted headstage and gives digital control of stimulation and subsequent feedback based on the elicited response. This latest software upgrade provides a 24/7 interactive multi-animal platform that can automate interactions with the CNS to shape CNS plasticity according to the investigator’s instructions. It provides: acquisition, processing, and storage of signals; continuous evaluation of the state of the CNS, which then triggers appropriate feedback under tight real time constraints; and a user interface to guide setup and tuning. The system also accommodates: multiple animals at a time to give sufficient throughput; flexible configuration to support different plasticity-inducing protocols; and automation and other new features. It enables long-term 24/7 studies that facilitate development of effective new therapies for enhancing functional recovery for people with chronic neuromuscular disorders.
