Main Abstract: Hyperreflexia is a main contributor to spasticity after neurological injury resulting in impairments and dysfunction in affected subjects. In chronically implanted rats, tibial nerve stimulation is used to elicit H-reflexes that are quantified from soleus EMG recordings.  In this study, rats[YS14.1] underwent HROC with either contingent VNS delivery or standard food-rewarded conditioning. A no-reward control group is included to determine whether changes in H-reflex amplitude depend on contingent reinforcement. Contingent CLV is delivered through a vagal cuff exclusively following successful down-conditioning trials, preserving the reinforcement contingency central to HROC, and then compared with the other experimental groups to observe overall change. Primary outcome measures include the rate of acquisition of H-reflex down-conditioning and the final magnitude of H-reflex suppression achieved during training. We hypothesize that rats receiving temporally contingent CLV during HROC will exhibit faster acquisition and greater reductions in H-reflex amplitude than rats receiving standard HROC alone. If no significant change occurs between the food-rewarded HROC group and contingent VNS group, we will add additional experimental VNS groups (Random VNS, Delayed VNS, Yoked VNS, etc.) to investigate the reinforcing effects of VNS. This study directly tests whether temporally contingent CLV enhances reinforcement-dependent spinal plasticity during H-reflex operant conditioning, providing a potential strategy for improving interventions targeting hyperreflexia associated with spasticity.

Mechanistic Abstract:
Mechanistic Abstract: Hyperreflexia is a main contributor to spasticity after neurological injury resulting in impairments and dysfunction in affected subjects. H-reflex operant conditioning (HROC) can reduce hyperreflexia through reflex down-conditioning, but its therapeutic effects require prolonged training timelines (50 days preclinically, ~ 3 months clinically) and has not been clinically deployed. Because HROC relies on reinforcement contingencies; we hypothesize that vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) can function as an artificial reinforcement signal that accelerates and amplifies reflex down-conditioning. Closed-loop vagus nerve stimulation (CLV) has been shown to enhance task-specific plasticity in multiple rehabilitation paradigms clinically. Here in a pre-clinical set-up, CLV is delivered only following successful down-conditioning trials, temporally linking neuromodulatory reinforcement from VNS delivery to the desired physiological outcome (20% H-reflex amplitude reduction). We hypothesize hyperreflexive rats receiving temporally contingent CLV during HROC will exhibit faster and greater reductions in H-reflex amplitude than rats receiving HROC alone. The VNS-enhanced H-reflex operant conditioning (VeHROC) may provide a strategy for improving interventions targeting hyperreflexia associated with spasticity.
