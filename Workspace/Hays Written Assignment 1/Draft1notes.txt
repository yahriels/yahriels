Before Paper 1 Title: Restoration of somatosensory function by pairing Vagus nerve stimulation with tactile rehabilitation
Authors: Michael J. Darrow, PhD, Tanvir M. Mian, MS, Monica Torres, BS, Zohra Haider, BS, Tanya Danaphongse, BS, Arash Seyedahmadi, BS, Robert L. Rennaker, PhD, Michael P. Kilgard, PhD, and Seth A. Hays, PhD
Year: 2020
Journal: Annals of Neurology
Role in Narrative: Established the initial proof-of-concept that pairing short bursts of VNS with mechanical tactile training restores somatosensory detection thresholds after chronic peripheral nerve injury. Left open whether high-volume, 6-week schedules were required or if shorter regimens could work.

Before Paper 2 Title: The timing and amount of vagus nerve stimulation during rehabilitative training affect poststroke recovery of forelimb strength
Authors: Seth A. Hays, PhD, Navid Khodaparast, PhD, David R. Hulsey, PhD, Robert L. Rennaker, PhD, and Michael P. Kilgard, PhD
Year: 2014
Journal: NeuroReport / Stroke
Role in Narrative: Demonstrated in motor stroke models that VNS efficacy depends non-linearly on timing and event pairing volume. Left open whether similar non-linear dosing constraints applied to somatosensory recovery. 

Before Paper 3 Title: The interval between VNS-tone pairings determines the extent of cortical map plasticity
Authors: Michael S. Borland, PhD, Navzer D. Engineer, PhD, William A. Vrana, BS, Nicole A. Moreno, BS, Sven Vanneste, PhD, and Michael P. Kilgard, PhD
Year: 2018
Journal: Neuroscience
Role in Narrative: Showed that short inter-stimulus intervals (e.g., 2 seconds) between VNS pairings fail to drive cortical map plasticity compared to 10-second intervals. Left open whether shortening session length or interval time in active rehabilitation would eradicate therapeutic gains due to neural desensitization.
